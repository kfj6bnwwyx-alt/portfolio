Privacy

Larder & List keeps your lists and recipes in your own iCloud. The developer collects nothing.

Updated November 2026

Where your things live

Your grocery list, recipes, week and settings are stored in your iCloud private database and sync between your devices through Apple’s CloudKit. They do not pass through any server of ours. There isn’t one.

When you share a household from Settings, your partner sees the shared data through CloudKit sharing on their own iCloud account. You can stop sharing at any time.

A recipe you import from a link, a post or photographed cookbook pages is stored in your iCloud database, with its text and photo. No copy is kept anywhere else.

What runs on your phone

The smart parts run on the device: splitting “2 eggplants and an orange” into two items, tidying an untidy recipe page, and estimating prep times. The text is never sent to the developer or to any other server for this.

What the developer collects

Nothing.

  • No analytics.
  • No ads and no ad tracking.
  • No accounts or passwords. The app uses your iCloud.
  • The developer cannot read your lists or recipes. Only you and the people you share a household with can.

Permissions the app asks for

iCloud
To sync between your devices and with a shared household.
Camera
To scan barcodes and photograph recipe pages.
Photo library
To add a photo to a recipe.
Notifications
To tell you when a timer has finished.
Siri
To add items when you ask, as in “Add onions to Larder & List”.

Turning any of these down only switches off that one feature. The rest of the app works as before.

If you email

If you write to brent.brooks@gmail.com, the developer has your address and what you wrote, and uses them to reply. Nothing else.

Children

The app collects no personal information from anyone, children included.

Changes
